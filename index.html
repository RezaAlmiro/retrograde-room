Every way to play a sound

A recording can be turned over along four axes: time, amplitude, frequency and space. Your four modes are the two mirrors of the waveform, in time and in amplitude. Rows marked added are dimensions you hadn't listed. Most rows have a button to try it or show it.

ATime the waveform's horizontal axis

Forward yours

Code P (prime): the sound as recorded.

Backward yours

Code R (retrograde): from the end to the start. Decays become swells and rising lines fall.

Ping-pong added

Forward to the end, then backward to the start, looping as a palindrome.

Backward grains added

Cut into 20–500 ms grains. Each grain plays backward but the grains keep their order, so rhythm survives and texture turns inside out.

Speed added

Tape style, 0.25× to 2×. Slower is lower, faster is higher.

Crab canon added

A melody played against its own retrograde at the same time. Bach wrote one in the Musical Offering.

BAmplitude the waveform's vertical axis

Flipped yours

Code I (inversion): every sample multiplied by −1. On its own it sounds exactly like forward.

Backward and flipped yours

Code RI: the waveform turned through 180°. You hear the reversal; the flip stays hidden.

Cancellation added

An upright and a flipped copy on the same spot add up to silence. Pull them apart and the sound returns, filtered.

One ear flipped added

Out of phase: the sound turns wide and hollow and seems to sit inside your head. Headphones only.

CFrequency the spectrum's axis

Spectral mirror added

Lows and highs swap around a mirror frequency, like an old radio scrambler. This is the closest match to "inversion" in twelve-tone music, where the intervals flip.

DSpace where the sound is, and where you are

Direction yours

Left, right, front and back. Drag a sound across the floor.

Height added

Above or below ear level. Shift-drag, or use the Height slider.

Distance added

Far sounds are quieter, and duller after travelling through air.

Motion paths added

Still, orbit, pass-by, through your head, rise and fall, wander.

Doppler added

Pitch rises as a sound comes toward you and drops as it leaves.

Front or back added

The hardest pair for ears to tell apart. A sound passes straight through your head.

Facing and beam added

A sound can point at you, away from you, or along its path, wide or narrow.

Room added

Open air, small room, studio, hall, cathedral, canyon.

Mirror the space added

Left ↔ right, front ↔ back, up ↔ down. The spatial twins of backward and flipped.

Big word on the stage added

The word being heard floats in front of you in the room, big, one word at a time, above the balls. Like the waveform card, it turns round for backward, so you read it mirrored, and upside down for flipped. Drag it anywhere; this browser remembers where.

Why flipped sounds the same

A flipped wave has exactly the same frequencies at the same strengths; only the sign of every sample changes. Hearing works mostly from those frequencies, so with nothing to compare against, the flip is almost always inaudible. Next to an upright copy the difference is total, because the two cancel. That is why the flip shows up in the cancellation and one-ear experiments, and why backward-and-flipped (RI) sounds like plain backward (R).

Not in this version

  • Pitch without speed, and speed without pitch. Needs a granular or phase-vocoder engine.
  • Reverse reverb. The room's tail arriving before the sound; needs an offline render.
  • Recording what you hear. The recorder takes the microphone only, not the sound of the room.
  • Recording inside claude.ai. The microphone is blocked there for every page of this kind. Use the recording version, or load a file.
  • Head tracking and speaker arrays. Ambisonics export and multi-speaker layouts.

How to use the room

A short manual. Open a section to read it; each Show button takes you to the control it describes.

What does this do?

Point at any button, slider or switch and wait a moment: a short note says what it does. With the keyboard, move to it with Tab. On a phone or tablet, press and hold a control, or turn on Tap to explain.

1Getting started
  1. Put on headphones. Your brain finds front, back and above from tiny differences between your two ears, and only headphones keep the ears apart. Speakers still play everything else.
  2. Press Start listening on the stage. Set Volume low first and bring it up slowly.
  3. Pick a scene from the Scene menu at the top. Each one is a small ready-made experiment with a note on what to listen for. “Start here” has a music box circling your head. The with menu beside it plays the scenes with a sound of your own.
  4. Click a ball, or its chip under the stage, to select that sound. The Sound tab then shows its controls.
  5. Try the Playback grid in the Sound tab: P forward, R backward, I flipped, RI backward and flipped.
2The stage

The stage is the room seen in 3D. The dark head in the middle is you. Its nose points to your front, and the blue wedge on the floor shows where you are facing. FRONT, BACK, LEFT and RIGHT are written on the floor; the rings are 2, 4, 8 and 16 metres from you. Thin lines outline the room you pick in the World tab.

Each ball is a sound. Rings spread out of a sound playing forward and close in on a sound playing backward. Dashed rings mean the sound is flipped. A faded ball is paused or muted.

The big word floating in front of you is the word the selected sound is saying, when it is a recording with a transcript (section 5). It is made of 3D letters in the colour of its ball, and sits ahead of you and above the balls. Drag it to put it somewhere else, or click it for handles.

Views

Orbit: drag empty space to swing round the room; scroll or pinch to zoom. Top: straight down, with your front at the top of the screen. Behind you: the camera rides behind your head and turns when you turn.

Moving things

Move a sound: drag its ball across the floor. Lift it: hold Shift (or Alt) while you drag, or use Height in the Sound tab.

Move the big word: drag it by its letters, the same way; Shift-drag lifts or lowers it. It keeps its place in front of you when you walk or turn, and this browser remembers it. A ball or your head in front of the word always wins, so you never grab the word by mistake. Click the word and it gets handles to move, turn and resize it (section 5). Put the word back, in the Transcript block, returns it.

Walk: drag your head across the floor, or use W A S D. Turn with Q and E, or the arrow keys. Back to the middle, in the World tab, puts you back.

Your own sounds: drop an audio file onto the stage, or use + Add sound, then From an audio file. A file stays in this browser tab; nothing is uploaded. You can also record a sound here: see Recording your own sound, below.

Snap

Turn on Snap (top left of the stage, or the G key) and a ball you drag lands on the lines of the floor: a whole number of metres from you, on one of 16 spokes from straight ahead round to behind you. Lift a ball with Snap on and it stops every half metre. Guide lines and a small label show where it will land, so several sounds can share a ring (the same distance) or a spoke (the same direction). Turn Snap off to place sounds freely.

Recording your own sound

There are three ways to start: choose Record your voice… under + Add sound, press Record… beside Load file… in the Sound tab, or choose Record… in a Try with menu. While you record, a card on the stage shows a red dot, the time (0:27 / 5:00), a level bar, and two buttons: Cancel and Stop and add. A recording can be up to five minutes long. The room pauses while you record and carries on afterwards.

The sound is recorded as it is, with no voice clean-up, so it suits a voice or an instrument. It arrives as a new ball called Recording 1, Recording 2 and so on, 2.5 metres in front of you, selected and playing. Its transcript appears by itself, as for any loaded file: English, Arabic, or a Quran recitation.

Where recording works. Inside claude.ai the microphone is blocked for every page of this kind, and nothing in the app can change that. Pressing Record there shows a note with the button Open the recording version. That is the same app on its own address, where the browser can ask you for the microphone: rezaalmiro.github.io/retrograde-room. There, the browser asks the first time you press Record; choose Allow.

Your recordings

A recording stays in this browser, on this device, with its transcript, until you delete it. Open + Add sound: your recordings are listed under Your recordings, newest first. Click one to put it into the room. Beside each one:

  • Rename it.
  • Save it as an audio file on this device.
  • Delete it for good. It asks first.

Each address and each browser keeps its own recordings. Those made on the recording version are kept there, not on claude.ai, and not on another device or in another browser. Clearing the browser's site data removes them. Files you load are still not kept, and the room itself (balls, positions, settings) starts fresh when the page reloads.

Try with your own sound

Try with, at the top of the Spec tab, and with, beside the Scene menu in the top bar, are the same choice: one of your own sounds, a kept recording, Load a file…, Record…, or the built-in sounds again. After that, every Try button and every scene plays with the sound you chose.

3Controlling each sound

Each sound has a chip under the stage. The selected sound also has the same three buttons floating beside its ball:

  • Play or pause just this sound (key P). Pressing play while nothing else plays starts only this one. A hollow dot on the chip means paused.
  • Mute (key M). The sound keeps running in time, silently, so it is still in step when you unmute it.
  • Take out (key Delete). The sound leaves the room but keeps every setting.

Put back: open + Add sound. Sounds you took out wait under Back into the room. The bin deletes one for good. Up to 12 can wait there; after that the oldest goes.

Play all and Pause all (top bar, or Space) start or stop everything. Restart together sends every sound back to its beginning and starts them all at the same instant: use it when sounds have drifted out of step.

Sync

Sync (top left of the stage) makes chosen sounds play at the same moment. It opens a list of the sounds in the room: tick two or more, or click their balls while the list is open. Synced sounds line up and share one playhead: play or pause one, jump it to another point on its waveform card, or change how it plays, and the others follow, so they stay in step. A dashed ring under each ball, which turns while they play, and sync on their chips mark them. From the start starts them again from the beginning, together; Unsync all lets them go.

In the Sound tab, S solos the sound (you hear only soloed sounds), M mutes it and ⧉ adds a copy beside it. The room holds up to eight sounds at once.

4The Sound tab

Everything here belongs to the selected sound.

Name and recording

Click the name to rename it. The menu under it picks the recording this ball plays; Load file… puts one of your own files into the ball instead. Record… makes a new sound with the microphone; it comes in as a ball of its own (section 2).

Playback grid

Four columns for time: Forward, Backward, Ping-pong (forward, then backward, looping) and Grains (short pieces, each backward, in their usual order). Two rows for the wave: Upright and Flipped. Keys 1 to 4 pick a column; F flips. Grain size appears when Grains is on.

Waveform and speed

The card shows the whole recording. It turns round for backward and upside down for flipped; click it to jump to a point. Under it: the playhead time, the direction and the speed. Live wave shows the wave as it plays. Speed works like tape: slower is lower, faster is higher.

Transcript

The words of a loaded recording, with the word you are hearing lit here and floating big on the stage. Section 5 explains it.

Making the flip audible

Flip one ear only turns the wave upside down in one ear, so the sound spreads wide and hollow (headphones only). Hear the flip adds an opposite twin on the same spot: the two cancel to silence until you drag one away.

Spectral mirror

Swaps low and high pitches around the Mirror frequency, like an old radio scrambler.

Position and motion

The three boxes say how far away, which way and how high the sound is. Height lifts it (0 is ear level). Six paths: Still, Orbit, Pass-by, Through you, Rise & fall and Wander. Path speed and Path size shape the path.

Which way it faces

A sound can point at you, away from you, or along its path. Beam narrows it: all round sounds the same from every side, while a narrow beam is loud in front and quiet behind. The cone on the stage shows the beam.

Level

Volume is this sound's own level. Room amount is how much of it reaches the room's echo.

5The transcript

Load or record speech, singing or Quran recitation, and the Transcript under its waveform shows the words. The word you are hearing is lit, whatever the sound is doing: played backward, the light runs backward through the text, and ping-pong, grains, speed and Doppler move it too. The box scrolls by itself to keep that word in view.

On and off

Use the switch beside Transcript, or the T key. Built-in sounds have no words: use Load file…, drop a file on the stage, record your own voice, or press Try a spoken sample for a short poem read aloud.

Language

Auto first listens for a Quran recitation, then works out which language is spoken. If it guesses wrong, choose English or Arabic.

Quran recitations

A recitation is matched to its sura and shown in the sura's own text, in the Uthmani script of the Madinah mushaf, with each ayah numbered. The basmala, and the isti'adha when it is recited, sit on lines of their own. A word the reciter repeats lights up again each time.

Other speech

English, Arabic and other speech are written out by a speech recogniser. Clear speech comes out well; singing, echo or music behind the voice can make it mishear a word.

Jumping and marks

Click a word to jump there; synced sounds jump with it. Small marks along the waveform card show where words begin, and taller ones where an ayah or a new passage begins. The shaded band on the card is the word you are hearing.

The big word on the stage

The word you are hearing from the selected sound also floats in front of you on the stage, big, and the one before it fades away. It is made of real 3D letters with depth and rounded edges, in the colour of its ball, with a soft shadow on the floor. The letters are traced from the browser's own drawing of the word, so Arabic and Quran script keep their exact shapes. While the word faces you, it turns slowly a little to each side, so you can see the depth of the letters.

Like the waveform card, the word turns round while the sound plays backward (in ping-pong, on the way back too), so you read it mirrored, and upside down when the sound is flipped; the text in the box stays readable. Turn it on or off with Big word on the stage or the B key. It floats ahead of you and above the balls; drag it by its letters to slide it along the floor (Shift-drag lifts or lowers it).

The look of the word

Three looks, under Big word on the stage in the Sound tab. Solid 3D is the one it starts with: solid letters. Glass 3D has see-through letters with bright edges. Flat is the see-through flat word of earlier versions. On a slow device the word goes back to Flat by itself, and says so. This browser remembers the look.

Handles on the word

Click the word: arrows appear on it and a small bar under it, as in a 3D program such as Blender.

  • Move: drag an arrow to slide the word along one line only. Red is left and right, green is up and down, blue is nearer and farther. The small squares between the arrows move it in two directions at once.
  • Turn: drag a ring to turn the word about one axis. Turning it by hand switches Face me off.
  • Size: drag a box to make the word bigger or smaller, from 0.4 to 3 times.
  • Face me: on, the word always faces you, wherever you look from. Off, it keeps its own direction, so you can look at it from the side or from behind.
  • Put it back: the usual place and the usual size, facing you. Put the word back in the Sound tab does the same; it shows once the word has been moved, turned or resized.

The × on the bar, the Esc key, or a click on the empty stage puts the handles away. Dragging the word by its letters still works without them. A ball or your head in front of the word always wins the click. This browser remembers where the word is, its size and its direction.

The first time

The page downloads its speech engine once, about 50 MB for Quran recitation and 80 MB more for other speech, and keeps it in this browser, so next time it starts at once. Everything runs on this device: your recording is never uploaded. Only the first five minutes of a file are used.

6The World tab

Everything here is shared by all the sounds.

Room

Six spaces, from Open air (no echo) to Cathedral (an echo of over five seconds) and Canyon (separate echoes from far walls). Room mix sets how loud the echo is.

Distance

How fast sounds fade as they move away, and Air absorption, which makes far sounds duller, as in real air.

Doppler

Pitch rises as a sound comes toward you and drops as it moves away, like a passing siren. Exaggerate makes it stronger.

Mirror the space

Swaps left and right, front and back, or up and down around your head. They can be combined. A faint ghost marks where each sound really is.

You

Turn your head, or go Back to the middle, facing front.

7P, R, I and RI

The names come from twelve-tone music. P (prime) is the sound as recorded. R (retrograde) plays it backward. I (inversion) turns the wave upside down: every sample is multiplied by −1. RI does both, which turns the waveform through 180°.

Why flipped sounds the same. A flipped wave has the same frequencies at the same strengths; only the sign of every sample changes. Your ears work mostly from those frequencies, so on its own the flip cannot be heard, and RI sounds just like R.

How to hear it anyway. Put a flipped copy beside an upright one: on the same spot they cancel to silence (Hear the flip, in the Sound tab). Flip one ear only and the sound turns hollow. The Four mirrors scene plays P, R, I and RI from four corners.

In twelve-tone music, inversion flips the intervals, so rising lines fall. The nearest thing here is the Spectral mirror.

9Troubleshooting

No sound. Check that you pressed Start listening, that Volume at the top is up, and that your headphones are connected and turned up. Check the sound is not paused or muted, and that no other sound is on solo. On an iPhone or iPad, make sure the side switch is not set to silent.

Flipping changes nothing. That is expected: see P, R, I and RI above.

The twin does not cancel. The two copies cancel exactly only while both sit still on the same spot with the same settings. Moving one, a motion path, Doppler, a different speed or mode, or pausing one of them breaks it. Restart together lines them up again, and syncing the pair keeps them lined up.

Front and back sound alike. That is normal: it is the hardest pair for ears. Move the sound or turn your head with Q and E; movement helps the brain decide.

Sounds are out of step. Press Restart together, or sync them so they stay together.

Pausing one sound pauses others. They are synced. Open Sync at the top left of the stage and untick it.

A sound has gone. Look in + Add sound, under Back into the room.

The 3D view is missing. Some browsers block 3D graphics. Every sound and control still works from the panels; another browser, or turning on hardware acceleration in your browser's settings, usually brings it back.

A file will not load. Use WAV, MP3, M4A or OGG. Only the first five minutes are used, and the file stays in this tab only.

The microphone is blocked. Inside claude.ai no page of this kind may use the microphone. Open the recording version, the same app on its own address, and record there. Or record on your phone and drop the file onto the stage.

The browser did not ask, or I said no. On the recording version, click the icon at the left end of the address bar and set Microphone to Allow. Then reload the page and press Record again. On a Mac, also look in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone.

My recordings are not here. Recordings are kept per address, per browser and per device. Those made on the recording version are listed there, not on claude.ai, and not in another browser or on another device. Clearing the browser's site data removes them.

The transcript shows no words. Built-in sounds have none: load a file or record your voice. If your file shows nothing, or the wrong words, choose English or Arabic instead of Auto. Music, echo or several voices at once make words harder to catch.

The transcript takes a while. The first time, it downloads its speech engine (about 130 MB at most), once. After that it starts at once, and a minute of recording takes a few seconds.

Everything has reset. The room starts fresh each time the page reloads: balls, positions and settings are not kept, and neither are loaded files. This browser does keep your recordings, the big word's place, size, direction and look, and whether the Transcript and the Big word are on.
